Backtest research log · H-001 → H-016

More search effort made the tuner surer, and its results worse.

Sixteen experiments testing whether a dip-avoidance trading strategy can beat buying and holding. The newest, H-016, doubled the optimiser's search budget to find out whether the tuner was simply not looking hard enough — then widened the search space when that failed. Neither helped, and both point at the same culprit.

02 — H-016

Result in

The tuner converged. The returns didn't follow.

H-016 asked one question in two halves: with twice the search effort, does the training-score disagreement between random seeds shrink, and does the live-result disagreement shrink? The log recorded a prediction in advance — that training spread would collapse while live spread would not. That is what happened.

Panel A — in-sample

Training-score spread narrowed

How far apart three seeds land on the same training window, averaged over 8 windows. MSFT's disagreement fell 2.2× — and measured against a typical score, from 56% down to 16%. The tuner is now finding much the same answer whichever way it starts.

Panel B — out-of-sample

Live-result spread widened

How far apart the same three seeds land on live excess annualized return. Shown for the no-transition-policy arm; the grandfather arm moved the same way (JPM +2.58, MSFT +3.36). All four arms widened — none narrowed.

MSFT JPM Panels share a 0–25 grid but carry different units — compare direction, not height.

Verdict — recorded in EXPERIMENT_LOG.md

Search depth is not the bottleneck — the objective is. The tuner got measurably better at the job it was given, and the results got worse. A model that fits its training window more consistently is not finding settings that survive contact with new data.

This is the fourth independent sighting of the same signature. H-002 found that more tuning effort halved the apparent advantage; H-010 found a 10× harder search made two stocks worse, not better; the H-016 diagnostic found seeds that fit the training window best performed worst live (ρ = −0.60); and now a controlled doubling reproduces it with both halves measured separately. The follow-on work belongs on what the tuner is asked to maximise and how it is validated — not on bigger populations.

One qualification, from the search-space arm in section 03: this conclusion is drawn inside the default bounds. Given a wider space to cover, the extra budget did help. "Search depth is not the bottleneck" may be a statement about this particular space rather than about depth as such.

03 — Search space

JPM · 9 new runs

Widening the search made it worse — and fixing the bad bounds didn't rescue it

If deeper search isn't the bottleneck, maybe the space is too small. There was real evidence for that: under the default bounds, three of the four JPM runs pinned the short EMA to exactly 2 — the floor of its own allowed range — in seven of eight windows. A parameter parked on its own bound usually means the bound is binding. So the bounds were widened by hand and the cell re-run.

JPM · seed 1111 · grandfather · pop 16 / gen 8

The one cell run under all three bound sets

The corrected rerun repaired every invalid bound — RSI back inside 0–100, long EMA back under the training window, stop loss to a sane 3–25% — and still finished 2.2 points below the run with the broken bounds, and 7.9 below the default-bounds baseline. Fixing the bounds did not recover the loss.

Three bounds were outside the physically valid range

--rsi-overbought-bounds 51 6994 — almost certainly a typo for 69 94. RSI is capped at 100 by construction, so only 50 of those 6,944 integers can ever bind: 99.3% of the range is dead. Every window of every run landed in the dead zone (188 to 6503) with the filter switched on but unable to fire, and since all dead values score identically there is no gradient back out. One of eight genes became free noise.

--long-ema-bounds 30 600 — a 1.0Y lookback is only ~250 trading days, but runs selected spans of 555, 484, 475, 392 and 325. ewm(span=…, adjust=False) has no min_periods, so those return a near-flat line anchored to the first price rather than erroring.

--stop-loss-bounds 1 50 — one run's eight windows ran 1.4%, 37.1%, 48.6%, 15.3%, 17.3%, 50.0%, 14.7%, 13.5%. At JPM's volatility a 50% stop never triggers and a 1.4% stop triggers constantly.

But these defects are not the explanation. That is what the corrected rerun tested, and it came back worse. The honest reading is that widening the space itself is what hurts here — the invalid bounds were real bugs worth fixing, not the cause.

Per-fund read — matched seed pairs, 16/8 minus 8/4

The two funds tell different stories, and neither is "deeper search works." On JPM the deeper search is consistently worse: 5 of 6 matched pairs deteriorated (mean −4.45 pts), and the experiment win rate fell to 0 of 6. All six JPM runs at 16/8 also converged onto an identical 41-trade shape — the tuner now agrees on what to do, and what it agrees on loses.

On MSFT the average is nearly flat (mean −0.68) but the seed pairs are violent: seed 2222 improved by +21 points (−16.6 → +5.0) while seed 999 deteriorated by −20 points (+3.1 → −17.3). The best single run in the whole matrix (MSFT +6.16) does come from the 16/8 budget — but it is seed 2222 flipping from the bad mode to the good one while another seed flipped the opposite way. That is the bimodal signature H-014 documented, reshuffled by the budget change, not a fund that responds to deeper search.

07 — The road here

Sixteen experiments, one durable conclusion

The standing bottom line, unchanged since H-004: no version of this strategy reliably beats buying and holding under a fair, capital-matched comparison. Three results looked like exceptions and each dissolved under stress-testing.

H-001
Stricter exit / re-entry confirmationRejected

Worse in 5 of 6 comparisons. Waiting for trend confirmation means waiting until the recovery has already happened — it missed more of the rebound, not less.

H-002
Was the MSFT winner just luck?Rejected

10 of 10 reruns beat buy & hold — but the benchmark itself was falling, and doubling the tuning effort halved the advantage. Initially accepted, then overturned by H-003.

H-003
Same setup, different recheck scheduleOverturned H-002

Changing only the retune cadence turned 8-of-8 into a coin flip: 5 of 8 at six months, 4 of 8 at three. The win was an accident of one time window.

H-004
Portfolio-wide review of 357 runsHuman review

No stock or setting both beat buy & hold and made money and held up across seeds and schedules. The best setup in the project succeeded 38% of the time.

H-005
Let the strategy invest more aggressivelyRefine & retest

Apple beat the market 3 of 3 at up to 1.35× exposure. The best result the project had produced — and the one that prompted the no-leverage rule.

H-006
Stress-testing the Apple resultDisqualified

It survived the schedule test that broke H-002 — but the accept was later retracted under the rule that the strategy may never deploy more capital than buy & hold.

H-007
The fair rematch: same $10k, no leverageRejected

Capped at buy & hold's capital, the "improved" strategy won 2 of 24 instead of 15 of 24. The leverage was the edge; there was no skill underneath it.

H-008
Twenty years, crashes includedRejected

Lost on return 15 times out of 15, by about 9%/yr — even with the 2020 crash in the window. It did cut worst-case drawdown in 14 of 15.

H-009
Penalise missing the reboundAccepted (MSFT)

The first fair, no-leverage change that genuinely improved a stock. Helped on choppy MSFT across all three seeds; did nothing for smoothly-rising AAPL and GOOGL.

H-010
Does a 10× harder search crack the risers?Rejected

It got worse, not better — a tighter fit to each training stretch generalised worse out of sample. The first clear overfitting signature, and a direct precursor to H-016.

H-011
Give it a real crash to dodgeRejected

The higher-returning the stock, the worse it did. Nvidia missed 162% of upside after its exits. Dip-dodging only pays where there is little upside to miss.

H-012
Deep MSFT optimisationSuperseded

Folded into the transition and tuning work that became H-013 onward.

H-013
Grandfather positions across retunesAccepted (3M only)

Nine better, two worse, one tied over 12 matched pairs (+0.98 avg, p = 0.066). The original "phantom exit" theory was wrong — zero fired in 58 switchovers. At a 1-month offset the benefit is worth nothing (+0.05 over nine pairs, p = 0.94); an earlier three-pair reading of −2.20 was retracted.

H-014
Warm-start each retune from the lastRejected

Failed its own stability test (spread 11.89). Results proved bimodal, not noisy — warm starting preserves the first roll of the dice rather than damping it. Six tests agree.

H-015
Retune monthly instead of quarterlyRejected

Looked like the project's first positive average excess return, on three seeds. Replication killed it: pooled across nine runs it is −2.35, with only 2 of 9 beating buy & hold. The original three seeds were the lucky ones.

H-016
Is the tuner actually finding the best answer?Rejected as a route

Yes — and it did not help. Training-score disagreement shrank; live spread widened in all four arms, the experiment win rate halved from 6/12 to 3/12, and mean excess fell from −2.48 to −5.04. A follow-on arm widened the JPM search space instead: worse in 8 of 8 pairs, and a corrected rerun with every invalid bound repaired came back worse again.

08 — Method

How to read this, and what it can't tell you

One data vintage

All 24 runs traded byte-identical files (MSFT-3Y.csv b442af0a…, JPM-3Y.csv bce531ed…). Derived slices have silently rolled forward before and invalidated three earlier comparisons, so the hash is checked, not assumed.

Three seeds is thin

Three seeds per arm sets a noise floor wider than most effects this project chases. The direction of travel is consistent across all four arms and both funds, which is the strongest part of the result; the magnitudes are not precise.

Training score ≠ live return

Panel A is the GA's own fitness on the training window; Panel B is realised excess return on data the tuner never saw. They share a grid for readability only — the units are unrelated.

Two win rates, kept apart

"Experiment win rate" counts runs that beat the benchmark; "trade win rate" counts profitable round trips inside a run. Section 04 defines both — they disagree sharply here, and only the first is a verdict.

Buy & hold sits outside the fence

The generic profile forces an active stop-loss (bounds 8–15%) and drawdown exit (2.5–4.0%); disabling either requires a value ≥100, which the search cannot reach. On a +37.5%/yr riser like this JPM window, the best answer — just hold — is literally outside the searchable space, so JPM's 0/6 may be a fence effect as much as a tuner effect. The generic-bh-reachable profile exists to test exactly this.

No leverage anywhere

Every run in this report holds exposure at or below 1.0×. The standing rule disqualifies any result that beats the benchmark by deploying more capital than it does.

One cell only

1.0-year lookback, 3-month offset, generic profile, Adj Close. H-013 showed the transition policy reverses sign at a 1-month offset; nothing here should be assumed to hold at other cells.

Bounds now join the resume identity

The search-space bounds were not part of run_grid.ps1's completed-run key, so a hand-launched wide-bounds run counted as "done" for a cell a default-bounds sweep wanted, and would have been silently skipped. The four profile-independent bounds are now checked on every resume; exit-gene bounds are checked when the sweep overrides them.

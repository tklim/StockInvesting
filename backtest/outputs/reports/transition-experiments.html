3. Why "inherit less" doesn't fix it — the damage is fat-tailed

Every pair at a quarter-inheritance, versus its own cold-start twin. Most are harmless and three improve — but four collapse, the worst by 19 points. Inheriting less made disaster rarer, not smaller. For a trading strategy that is worse than a steady small loss. Win rate here is 41.7% (5 of 12) — near a coin flip, yet the average is −4.36 because the losses are so much larger than the wins.

4. Monthly re-tuning — a lead that did not survive replication RETRACTED

Three MSFT seeds at a 1-month offset averaged +2.07, which looked like the first configuration in this whole project to beat buy & hold. Adding three more MSFT seeds and a second stock killed it: the pooled average is −2.35, and only 2 of 9 runs are positive. The original three simply happened to be the lucky ones.

MSFT JPM (independent check) rings mark the original three seeds

Third time in this project a small-sample lead has evaporated on replication (see also H-002 and the first grandfather write-up). The caution about annualizing ~21-day windows still stands.

4b. Grandfather works at quarterly offsets, not monthly

The same paired test at both re-tuning frequencies. At a 3-month offset grandfather is worth about a point and wins 9 of 12; at 1 month the effect vanishes to essentially zero. It does not backfire — it simply stops helping, which fits the mechanism: at monthly offsets a position spans many windows, so its original exit rules persist far longer than intended.

An earlier 3-pair sample suggested grandfather was actively harmful at monthly offsets (−2.20). With all 9 pairs that reverses to +0.05 — the harm was noise, but so was the benefit.

5. Why a single run proves nothing

The strategy's result swings wildly depending on the tuner's random starting point. Run the exact same settings six times with six different random seeds and you get six very different answers. Grandfather's real benefit is about 1 point — roughly 20× smaller than that random swing. So comparing two single runs measures luck, not policy. The fix is to compare each seed against itself.
